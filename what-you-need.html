Where a statement arrives, and where none does

Form 1099-DA, Digital Asset Proceeds From Broker Transactions, is new for the 2025 tax year. Gross proceeds reporting is required for transactions effected on or after January 1, 2025, and cost basis reporting begins for certain transactions effected on or after January 1, 2026. This is why the checklist above asks for your own exports rather than relying on the statements.

Form 1099-DA for the 2025 tax year, by where the activity happened
Where the activity happened Does a statement generally arrive? What that means for your records
US custodial exchange or hosted wallet provider Yes Generally gross proceeds only for 2025. Basis begins to appear for transactions effected on or after January 1, 2026.
Digital asset kiosk, or certain payment processors Yes These are also brokers required to file the form.
DeFi front end, or any decentralized or non-custodial broker No Decentralized and non-custodial brokers are excluded, so your own records are the only record.
Your own self-custody wallet No On-chain history read from the public address is the record.
Exchange based outside the United States Generally no Export the full transaction history yourself while you still have access to the account.
Staking and mining rewards No No Form 1099-DA is required for staking, and none arrives for mining rewards.

Whether a statement arrives does not change what is reported. Income, gain or loss from taxable transactions involving virtual currency must be reported regardless of the amount and regardless of whether a payee statement is received.

The same goes for the small-value thresholds. A broker may leave designated sales of qualifying stablecoins off the form where aggregate gross proceeds do not exceed $10,000 for the year, and specified NFT sales where aggregate gross proceeds do not exceed $600. Those are thresholds for broker reporting, not exclusions for the taxpayer: the IRS answers yes on stablecoin exchanges that do not appear on a Form 1099-DA, and yes on NFT dispositions under $600 that do not appear on one.

How to export your history from an exchange

Every venue lays this out differently and the screens change often, so this stays general. We do not name steps for a particular exchange and we do not link to any.

  • Look for the account area named for reports, statements, documents or taxes, rather than the trading screen.
  • Ask for the full transaction history for the whole calendar year. A summary, a profit and loss figure or a year-end balance is not enough to rebuild basis from.
  • Choose the CSV option where one exists. A PDF statement can be read, but it takes longer to reconcile.
  • Export each account, sub-account and year separately, and leave the file names as the venue writes them.
  • Where the export lets you choose what to include, include trades, conversions, deposits, withdrawals, fees and rewards.
  • Check the first and last rows before you send it. The file should begin on or before January 1 and end on or after December 31 of that year.
  • If the export is delivered by an email link, download the file yourself and send us the file, not the link.
  • Do not create an API key for us, and do not send screenshots in place of an export.

What a read-only record looks like

  • A public wallet address, never a seed phrase or a private key.
  • A CSV or PDF file you downloaded yourself, never an API key, a password or a login.
  • A statement you were sent, or a copy of a return you already filed.

If the only way to produce a record is to give someone access to your account, tell us instead. We work from what you can export, and where a gap remains we tell you what is missing and what it affects before any work starts.
