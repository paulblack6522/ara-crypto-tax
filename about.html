About Ara Tax Services LLC

We are a private tax preparation firm in San Ramon, California. We prepare and file United States federal tax reporting for people who have cryptocurrency activity.

What we do

You send us the records of what you did with digital assets during the year. We reconcile those records, work out the gain, loss and income they produce, and report the result on the federal forms it belongs on.

The forms that work usually lands on:

  • Form 1040, page 1. The 2025 Form 1040 asks, directly under Filing Status, whether at any time during 2025 you received a digital asset as a reward, award or payment, or sold, exchanged or otherwise disposed of one. Every individual filer answers it Yes or No, whether or not they hold digital assets.
  • Form 8949, Sales and Other Dispositions of Capital Assets. This covers every sale, exchange or other disposition of a digital asset held as a capital asset, including crypto-to-crypto swaps and paying for goods or services with crypto.
  • Schedule D. Summarises the Form 8949 totals.
  • Schedule 1, Part I, line 8v. The 2025 Schedule 1 has a dedicated line for digital assets received as ordinary income not reported elsewhere.
  • Schedule C and Schedule SE. These apply where the activity is carried on as a trade or business, such as mining as a trade or business, an independent contractor paid in crypto, or a business that sells goods or services for crypto.
  • Form 1040-X. Amends a return that was already filed, for example an unreported disposal, a wrong answer to the digital asset question, or a corrected Form 1099-DA.

The IRS treats digital assets as property rather than currency, and the general tax principles that apply to property transactions apply to them. That single rule is what turns a swap, a purchase paid for in crypto, and a gas fee paid in crypto into events that have to be worked out and reported.

This site is about federal reporting. The request form asks which state or states you were a tax resident of, and it asks in order to scope the engagement.

How we work

  1. You tell us the scope

    The request form asks six short questions: the tax years, the kinds of activity, roughly how many transactions, how many exchanges and wallets and which ones by name, and what you filed in prior years. It uses bands rather than exact figures wherever a band is enough. It collects no Social Security number, no date of birth, no bank or card details, no wallet balances, and no document upload.

  2. We look at your records before we quote

    Taxpayers have to keep records sufficient to establish the positions taken on their returns, including receipts, sales, exchanges, dispositions, transfers and fair market values. We read what you actually have first. If something needed is missing, we tell you what it is before any work starts, not halfway through.

  3. You get a written quote before work begins

    The quote is priced on the work involved: how many venues and chains are in play, how many transactions and disposals there are, and how much of your cost basis is documented. Nothing is charged until you accept the quote. We publish no prices on this site because a price written before we have seen the scope would be a number we made up.

  4. A person prepares the return

    Every return we prepare is worked and checked by a person. Our team includes experienced tax preparers and specialists who work with the IRS filing system, so a complex crypto return is handled by someone who does this work, not passed to software to finish. Software is used to import and match transactions; it is not left to decide the treatment of an item on its own and send the result out unread.

  5. You review a draft before anything is filed

    We walk you through the draft and the schedules behind it, show you which transactions produced which numbers, and answer questions on it. Nothing is filed until you have seen it and told us to go ahead.

How your records reach us

We ask for read-only exports and public wallet addresses. Read-only means a CSV or a transaction history file that can be looked at and nothing more. A public wallet address is the address anyone can already see on a block explorer.

We do not accept records by email. When work starts we set up a channel for the file transfer and tell you how to use it before you send anything. Do not attach returns, statements or exchange exports to an email, and do not put an account number, a password or a key in one.

What we will never ask for

We will never ask for your seed phrase, private keys, exchange passwords, or API keys. No one legitimate ever will.

We are not the IRS

Ara Tax Services LLC is a private tax preparation firm. We are not the IRS. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of the Internal Revenue Service, the U.S. Department of the Treasury, or any government agency.

You can file your own federal tax return directly with the IRS at no cost to you, including through IRS Free File if you are eligible. Our service is a paid, optional alternative in which we prepare your return for you. Nobody has to hire anyone to file a return. If your activity for the year is straightforward and you are comfortable reading your own records, doing it yourself is a reasonable thing to do, and we would rather say so here than have you find it out later.

The plain, boxy look of this site comes from a design system that is in the public domain. It carries no connection to any government agency, and we have kept every government identity element off these pages deliberately.

What we do not do

  • We do not take custody of your crypto. We never hold, move, trade or have any authority over your assets. We do not ask for a private key, a seed phrase, an exchange password or an API key, and there is nowhere on this site to enter one.
  • We do not give investment or trading advice. No coin recommendations, no price views, no buy, sell or hold opinions, no portfolio suggestions, and no ranking of exchanges or wallets. Where an exchange or a wallet is named on this site, it is named as an example of somewhere you may need to export records from.
  • We do not represent people in collection matters or negotiate amounts owed. Catching up on unfiled years and amending a return that was already filed are ordinary preparation work and we do them. Negotiating a balance is a different business and it is not ours.
  • We do not promise a result. We describe the work we do and the forms we file. Anyone telling you in advance what your number will be has not seen your records.
  • We do not publish prices here. You see a written quote after we have seen the scope, and nothing is charged until you accept it.

What we do not claim

You will not find a client count, a founding year, an award badge, a partner logo, a star rating or a quote from a client anywhere on this site. Nothing about a history has been invented to fill that space, and none of it is coming later either unless it is real.

What we can put in front of you instead is the process above: you see what is missing from your records before you commit, you see a written quote before any work starts, and you see a draft before anything is filed. Each of those is checkable by you, in order, before you have paid anything.
