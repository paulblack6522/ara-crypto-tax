Crypto tax filing, prepared and filed for you

Ara Tax Services LLC is a private tax preparation firm in San Ramon, California. We prepare United States federal tax returns for people who bought, sold, swapped, spent, earned or transferred digital assets during the year. You send us your exchange exports and wallet addresses, we reconcile them and prepare the return, and we file it once you have read it and approved it.

What this service is

  • We prepare and file your federal return covering digital-asset activity. That means Form 8949 and Schedule D for disposals, and Schedule 1 or Schedule C for digital assets received as income.
  • A preparer reads your records. We reconcile the exports and wallet histories you send us, and we tell you what is missing before we start work.
  • We are not the IRS. We are a private firm, and we are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of any government agency.
  • We do not give investment advice, we do not tell you what to buy or sell, and we never take custody of your assets, your keys or your accounts.

How it works, in three steps

The full sequence is set out on how it works. In short:

  1. Tell us about the year

    You complete a five-step intake form. It asks which tax years you need, which exchanges and wallets you used, what kind of activity you had, and what records you can provide. It asks for no passwords, no private keys and no account numbers.

  2. Send your records

    We reply with the scope of the work and a quote. Once you accept, you send read-only CSV exports and public wallet addresses. We reconcile them, and we come back to you with anything that is missing or cannot be matched.

  3. Review, then we file

    We prepare the return and the digital-asset forms that go with it. You read the draft, ask us anything about it, and tell us to file. Nothing is filed with the IRS until you approve it.

What we handle

Each of these is asked about on the intake form, because each one changes how the return is prepared. The statements below are the federal treatment, not advice about your own position.

  • Exchange trading

    Every sale, exchange or other disposition of a digital asset held as a capital asset goes on Form 8949, including crypto-to-crypto swaps, and the short-term and long-term totals carry to Schedule D.

  • Wallet-to-wallet transfers

    Moving digital assets between wallets, addresses or accounts you own is not a taxable event, unless digital assets are used or withheld to pay for the transfer service, in which case that portion is a disposition.

  • Staking rewards

    Rev. Rul. 2023-14 includes the fair market value of validation rewards in gross income in the year the taxpayer gains dominion and control over them, reported as ordinary income on Schedule 1, line 8v, or on Schedule C where it is a trade or business.

  • Mining

    Notice 2014-21 includes the fair market value of mined units in gross income as of the date of receipt; where mining is a trade or business the net earnings are subject to self-employment tax on Schedule C with Schedule SE, and where it is not, the income is ordinary income on Schedule 1.

  • NFTs

    NFTs are digital assets, so buying, selling and swapping them produces capital gain or loss on Form 8949 using the digital-asset boxes, and an NFT disposition under $600 that appears on no Form 1099-DA is still reportable.

  • DeFi

    An exchange of one digital asset for another that differs materially is a taxable disposition, and paying a gas fee in digital assets is itself a disposition. Notice 2024-57 lists wrapping, liquidity, staking, lending, short sale and notional principal contract transactions as categories requiring further study, and draws no inference about their treatment.

  • Airdrops and forks

    Rev. Rul. 2019-24 treats an airdrop of a new cryptocurrency following a hard fork as ordinary income at its fair market value when received, provided the taxpayer has dominion and control, and finds no gross income where a hard fork produces no new units.

  • Crypto received as income

    Digital assets paid as employee wages are subject to federal income tax withholding, FICA and FUTA and are reported on Form W-2 at fair market value on the date of receipt, while digital assets paid to an independent contractor are self-employment income on Schedule C with Schedule SE.

  • Losses

    Section 165 allows a loss only where a transaction is closed and completed, fixed by an identifiable event and actually sustained in the year, and an individual's net capital loss deduction is limited to $3,000 per year ($1,500 if married filing separately) with the excess carried forward.

New for the 2025 tax year: Form 1099-DA

Form 1099-DA, Digital Asset Proceeds From Broker Transactions, is new. Gross proceeds reporting is required for transactions effected on or after January 1, 2025, so the 2025 tax year is the first year the form exists. Brokers had to furnish the 2025 form to taxpayers by February 17, 2026.

The IRS states that most 2025 statements will not provide basis, and that basis must be calculated by taxpayers before their 2025 tax return can be filed. Cost basis reporting begins for certain transactions effected on or after January 1, 2026.

No Form 1099-DA arrives from DeFi front-ends, from non-custodial wallets, generally from exchanges outside the United States, or for staking and mining rewards. The reporting duty does not depend on receiving a statement: income, gain or loss from all taxable transactions involving virtual currency must be reported regardless of the amount or whether a payee statement arrives.
