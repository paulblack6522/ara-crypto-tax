How it works

This page sets out the whole engagement in order, from the first form you fill in to the copies you keep. Read it before you start so you know what we ask for and when.

The seven steps

Every engagement runs in the same order. You can stop at any step before you accept the quote, and nothing is charged before then.

  1. You complete the request form

    It has five short steps and asks about tax years, venues, activity types and records. You do not need any documents to hand yet. The form never asks for a Social Security number, a date of birth, a seed phrase, a private key, an exchange password or an API key.

  2. A preparer reviews it and contacts you

    A preparer reads your answers and replies by email or phone, whichever you asked for. If anything in your answers changes the scope, we ask about it then rather than later.

  3. You get a written quote before any work starts

    The quote states the tax years covered, the forms we will prepare, and what is outside the scope. Nothing is charged until you accept it in writing. This site carries no price list because the work is scoped from your own activity.

  4. You send your transaction records

    Exchange CSV exports, public wallet addresses and any statements you received go through a secure channel we set up for you, never by plain email. We ask for read-only records only. We do not ask for keys, passwords or API access, and we never take custody of your assets.

  5. We reconstruct basis and prepare the forms

    We rebuild cost basis from your records, reconcile transfers between wallets and accounts you own, and prepare Form 8949 and Schedule D along with the rest of your return. Ordinary income from digital assets goes to Schedule 1 or, where the activity is a trade or business, to Schedule C. If you want to see what each of these forms is for, our guide to the IRS forms a crypto return involves walks through them.

  6. You review a draft

    You get the full draft return with the digital asset schedules attached, plus a plain summary of how each figure was arrived at. Nothing is filed until you have read it and signed the filing authorization.

  7. We file, and you keep everything

    After filing you receive copies of every form filed and the working papers behind them, including the transaction ledger, the basis calculations and the source records we worked from. They are yours to keep.

What happens if your records are incomplete

Incomplete records are ordinary. Exchanges close, wallets get abandoned, and the statements that do arrive are often thinner than people expect. Here is what that means in practice and what we do about it.

A statement usually does not carry your basis

Form 1099-DA, Digital Asset Proceeds From Broker Transactions, is new for the 2025 tax year. Gross proceeds reporting is required for transactions effected on or after January 1, 2025, and cost basis reporting begins for certain transactions effected on or after January 1, 2026. The IRS states that most 2025 statements will not provide basis and that basis must be calculated by taxpayers before their 2025 tax return can be filed.

For a lot of activity, no statement arrives at all

The brokers who file the form are custodial brokers, hosted wallet providers, digital asset kiosks and certain payment processors. Decentralized and non-custodial brokers are excluded. No Form 1099-DA arrives from a DeFi front end, from a self-custody wallet, generally from a non-US exchange, or for staking and mining rewards. The reporting duty does not depend on receiving one: income, gain or loss from taxable transactions involving virtual currency must be reported regardless of the amount or whether a payee statement is received.

How we reconstruct what is missing

We work from what can be evidenced. Public wallet addresses give us on-chain history. Exchange exports give us trade, deposit and withdrawal records. Where a withdrawal from one venue and a deposit into another are the same units, we match them so that a transfer between wallets you own is not read as a sale and a repurchase. Where a gap remains, we tell you what is missing and what it affects before we start work, not after.

Where the rules decide the answer for you

Some gaps have a fixed consequence rather than a judgment call.

  • Basis and lot identification for acquisitions and dispositions of digital assets on or after January 1, 2025 are determined per wallet and per account, not across one pooled total.
  • With no adequate identification, the default is first in, first out within that wallet or account: for units held with a broker, the earliest units of that asset acquired in that broker's custody; for units not held with a broker, the earliest purchase date of units in that wallet.
  • For digital assets received as a gift, if no documentation of the donor's basis exists, basis is zero.
  • The Rev. Proc. 2024-28 safe harbor allocates unused basis to wallets and accounts, but it does not determine the amount of that basis. The amount still has to be substantiated.
  • For 2026 transactions, the acquisition date and basis a broker reports may not match the lot identification and basis on your own books. Under the relief in Notice 2026-20 that is expected, not an error.

Records are the taxpayer's own responsibility

The IRS requires taxpayers to keep records sufficient to establish the positions taken on their returns, including receipts, sales, exchanges, dispositions, transfers and fair market values. Part of the work is handing back a clean ledger you can keep for that purpose.

What we do not do

It is quicker to say this plainly than to let you find out later.

Outside the scope of this service

  • No investment advice. We do not tell you what to buy, sell or hold, we do not rate coins, exchanges or wallets, and we do not comment on prices.
  • No custody of assets or keys. We never hold your digital assets, and we never ask for a seed phrase, a private key, an exchange password or an API key.
  • No bookkeeping for a business unless you engage us for it separately. Preparing a return is not the same job as keeping the books behind it.
  • No representation before the IRS. Representation is a separate engagement and requires a practitioner authorized to provide it.

If what you need falls outside that list, say so on the request form and we will tell you before you accept a quote.
