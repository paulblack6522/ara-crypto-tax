The IRS forms a crypto tax return involves

The IRS treats digital assets as property, so cryptocurrency activity is reported on the same kinds of forms as other property — plus one new statement, Form 1099-DA. This page explains what each form is for and when it applies. It is general information, not advice for your situation. We prepare and file these for you.

In one line

Every filer answers a digital-asset question on Form 1040. Sales and swaps are reported on Form 8949 and carried to Schedule D. Crypto received as income goes on Schedule 1, or Schedule C if it is a business. From the 2025 tax year, US brokers also send you Form 1099-DA. We work out which of these your return needs.

The forms at a glance

Which forms apply depends on what you did during the year. A preparer confirms the set for your return.

Federal forms for digital-asset activity, what each is for, and when it applies
Form What it is When it applies
Form 1040 — digital asset question The question on page 1, directly under Filing Status, asking whether you received or disposed of a digital asset during the year. Every individual filer answers it Yes or No, whether or not they hold digital assets.
Form 8949 Sales and Other Dispositions of Capital Assets. Lists each sale, exchange or disposal, with proceeds, basis and gain or loss. Every sale, exchange or disposal of a digital asset held as a capital asset, including crypto-to-crypto swaps and paying for goods or services with crypto.
Schedule D (Form 1040) Capital Gains and Losses. Summarises the totals from Form 8949 into short-term and long-term gain or loss. Whenever Form 8949 is used for the return.
Schedule 1 (Form 1040) Additional Income and Adjustments to Income. The 2025 form has a dedicated line, Part I line 8v, for digital assets received as ordinary income. Crypto received as ordinary income and not reported elsewhere — for example staking rewards, or an airdrop you had dominion and control over.
Schedule C (Form 1040) Profit or Loss from Business. Net earnings are also subject to self-employment tax. Where the activity is a trade or business — mining as a business, an independent contractor paid in crypto, or a business that sells for crypto.
Form 1099-DA Digital Asset Proceeds From Broker Transactions. A statement your broker sends to you and the IRS. You receive this one; you do not file it. US custodial brokers issue it for dispositions from the 2025 tax year onward. See the section below.
Form 1040-X Amended U.S. Individual Income Tax Return. Corrects a return already filed. A past year that missed a disposal, answered the digital-asset question wrongly, or needs a corrected Form 1099-DA reflected. Generally within 3 years of filing or 2 years of paying the tax, whichever is later.
FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts, filed with FinCEN, not the IRS. Foreign financial accounts over $10,000 in aggregate at any time in the year. FinCEN Notice 2020-2 states its rules do not currently define an account holding only virtual currency as reportable — so a preparer asks what the account holds.
Form 8938 Statement of Specified Foreign Financial Assets, filed with the return. Above the reporting thresholds (for someone living in the US: more than $50,000 on the last day of the year or $75,000 at any time, higher if married filing jointly). A preparer asks about foreign-held assets; the IRS has not published guidance putting crypto specifically inside or outside this form.
Form 709 United States Gift Tax Return. A gift of digital assets above the annual exclusion, which is $19,000 per recipient for 2025.

Form 1099-DA — the new statement for 2025

Brokers use it to report the proceeds from your digital-asset dispositions — and in some cases the cost basis — to you and to the IRS. Gross proceeds reporting applies to transactions on or after January 1, 2025; cost basis reporting begins for certain transactions on or after January 1, 2026. Brokers had to furnish the 2025 form by February 17, 2026.

Most 2025 statements will not show your basis

In the IRS's own words, “You must calculate basis before you file your tax return.” Because 2025 statements generally show proceeds only, your cost basis usually has to be reconstructed from your own records — a large part of what a preparer does, and the step people most often get wrong on their own.

Where a form arrives, and where none does

  • Expect one from each US custodial broker — a hosted exchange or wallet provider, a digital-asset kiosk, or certain payment processors.
  • Expect none from a DeFi front-end, a non-custodial wallet, a non-US exchange, or for staking and mining rewards.
  • The duty to report does not depend on receiving a form. A missing form is not a reason a transaction goes unreported — it is a reason the records have to be rebuilt.

Cost basis is now tracked per wallet and per account

Under the 2024 final regulations, basis and lot identification for acquisitions and dispositions on or after January 1, 2025 are determined per wallet and per account, not across one universal pool. Revenue Procedure 2024-28 gave a safe harbor for allocating unused basis as of that date. In plain terms: if you moved crypto between your own wallets and exchanges, basis has to be assigned to the right wallet under a reasonable method.

How we handle the forms for you

We reconcile your exchange and wallet records, reconstruct cost basis where a 2025 statement does not show it, reconcile transfers between your own wallets, and prepare Form 8949 and Schedule D along with the rest of your return.
