Three things this data decides:
  1. Feb–Apr is the harvest — form 8949 runs ~11× its June floor at the March peak. A campaign launched now enters its learning phase in the cheap season and hits filing season already calibrated.
  2. October 15 is the nearest revenue window. Extension filers produce a real secondary bump (form 8949 Oct25: 12,100). "Behind on crypto filings?" is the message that matches it.
  3. 1099-DA is a live wave, not a legacy term. First forms ever were furnished Feb 2026; the next wave lands Jan–Feb 2027, and those forms start carrying cost basis. Confused recipients searching a form number they've never seen = the best lead profile in this campaign.

Skipped on purpose: fbar crypto, form 8938 cryptocurrency, schedule d crypto — all ~10 searches/mo or fewer. Bidding them directly starves the account of data; phrase match collects them anyway when they matter.

2 · Ad groups & keywords

Match types embedded — paste as-is. [exact] for the proven head terms, "phrase" to sweep the long tail into the search-terms report. No broad at launch.

Why there are zero certification keywords — deliberate, not an oversight. Keyword Planner shows real volume on crypto tax accountant (210/mo, $20.91), crypto cpa near me (210/mo, $19.58), cpa cryptocurrency (210/mo, $20.20), crypto accountant near me (210/mo). All excluded, and negatived in §4. A searcher typing CPA/accountant/EA is shopping for a credential the firm has not shown us it holds — the ad would misrepresent by context even with clean copy, and the click costs $20 to disappoint. The same logic keeps audit out: IRS audit representation legally requires CPA/EA/attorney status. If the firm later documents real credentials, these unlock as a third ad group — the demand is measured and waiting.

AG1 — IRS Crypto Declaration  DKI ✅ 19 keywords

Theme: "I have crypto and the IRS needs to know about it — someone do this for me." Head term crypto taxes (3,600/mo) carries the group; the two how-to phrases are kept deliberately — they're DIYers at the decision moment, and the ad's whole pitch is "stop, we'll do it".

AG2 — IRS Forms · 8949 / 1099-DA  DKI ✅ 15 keywords

Theme: a person holding a specific form number. form 8949 at 18,100/mo and $3.30 is this campaign's discovery engine — the §4 negatives strip the PDF-hunters and instruction-readers, and the search-terms report tells us in week one what the residue really is. 1099 da (6,600/mo) is the premium slice: people holding a form that has never existed before.

Scaling note (doctrine, not a launch task): exact and phrase ideally live in separate ad groups so each can be read cleanly. At this account's size we launch lean — 2 groups, both match types — and split the phrase sweepers out into their own groups once the search-terms report has enough volume to justify it.

4 · Negative keywords — one shared list, apply to the campaign

Four jobs in one list: (1) block every certification query (CPA/EA/accountant/attorney — see §2), (2) block DIY software and exchange-navigational traffic (people looking for Coinbase's tax page or TurboTax, not a preparer), (3) strip form-PDF hunters from AG2, (4) keep us out of the tax-relief restricted category (relief / settlement / penalty terms) and away from evasion queries.

Words checked as safe to block: none of these appear inside any positive keyword above (verified by script). help, irs, form, schedule, near me and year numbers are deliberately NOT negatived — they're load-bearing. back taxes is also left biddable: prior-year filers are a stated service segment. One deliberate tradeoff: the exchange-name negatives (coinbase, kraken…) also block future "coinbase 1099 da"-type recipient queries — no such query has measured volume yet, but re-check the search-terms report in January and carve out exceptions if that wave appears.
