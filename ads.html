Why there are zero certification keywords — deliberate, not an oversight. Keyword Planner shows real volume on crypto tax accountant (210/mo, $20.91), crypto cpa near me (210/mo, $19.58), cpa cryptocurrency (210/mo, $20.20), crypto accountant near me (210/mo). All excluded, and negatived in §4. A searcher typing CPA/accountant/EA is shopping for a credential the firm has not shown us it holds — the ad would misrepresent by context even with clean copy, and the click costs $20 to disappoint. The same logic keeps audit out: IRS audit representation legally requires CPA/EA/attorney status. If the firm later documents real credentials, these unlock as their own ad group — the demand is measured and waiting.

4 · Negative keywords — one shared list, apply to the campaign

Four jobs in one list: (1) block every certification query (CPA/EA/accountant/attorney — see §0), (2) block DIY software and exchange-navigational traffic (people looking for Coinbase's tax page or TurboTax, not a preparer), (3) block form-PDF hunters, (4) keep us out of the tax-relief restricted category (relief / settlement / penalty terms) and away from evasion queries. With a closed keyword list the negatives mostly guard the 5 phrase sweepers — cheap insurance.

Words checked as safe to block: none of these appear inside any positive keyword above (verified by script). help, irs, form, schedule, near me and year numbers are deliberately NOT negatived — they're load-bearing. back taxes is also left biddable: prior-year filers are a stated service segment. One deliberate tradeoff: the exchange-name negatives (coinbase, kraken…) also block future "coinbase 1099 da"-type recipient queries — no such query has measured volume yet, but re-check the search-terms report in January and carve out exceptions if that wave appears.
